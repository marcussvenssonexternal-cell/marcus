# TwelveLabs LinkedIn video ads: reference notes

Four TwelveLabs video ads shared as references (Oct 2026), broken down frame by frame. The videos
themselves are not stored here. All four are 16:9 (1280×720), 25–30 seconds, with sound; the audio
could not be transcribed, but every video tells its story without it. **Video 4 is the team's favourite.**

## What all four do

- **Open on the problem, shown rather than told.** The first 3–5 seconds make you feel the pain:
  an ad cutting into a car chase, or a cursor frantically scrubbing through footage.
- **One short line of text at a time**, black on white and centred, held for 2–3 seconds. About four lines in
  30 seconds in total.
- **Product as clean motion graphics, not screen recordings.** Rounded video cards, a filmstrip, a
  progress bar and labels in one accent colour (lime green), and a cursor that clicks things.
- **A concrete action as the payoff:** "+ ADD ADVERT" between SCENE END and SCENE START, or an
  "Export to social media" button turning green.
- **Plain-language labels** a person would use: "Rooftop confession", "Kitchen argument", "Weather".
- **Generic stock footage** (a police chase, a news studio), never recognisable films or shows.
- **Ending:** a payoff line, then 3–5 seconds of logo on a soft gradient. No CTA in the video; the CTA is
  LinkedIn's button.
- **Post copy:** a relatable pain line first, one line on the old way, one on what TwelveLabs does,
  three emoji bullets, and a short closing line (about 400 characters).

Why it works best:
- **The same search fails, then succeeds.** "No results found" is the most relatable moment in any of the four, and
  the fix is the same intent typed naturally.
- **One specific, concrete query** ("Linebacker sacks quarterback for a loss"), then breadth through many queries.
- **The closing line runs into the logo**, so the brand is the answer to the sentence.
- **The post goes after metadata itself**: "Tags capture what someone thought to write down. They don't capture
  what's actually happening on screen." It ends with a question to start comments: "What does your current
  video search actually look like? 👀"

### The motion that makes it feel good

The team likes video 4 most partly for its animation, which keeps lots of screens in motion:
- **Depth from the first frame:** the main window sits in front of blurred video screens.
- **The rushing wall:** a full-screen grid of very rounded thumbnails slides diagonally past the camera for about a second.
- **The pick:** the wall collapses into a row under an empty player; a click fills the player.
- **One card:** the query sits on top of the playing clip.
- **The pull-back:** the camera zooms out from that card over a canvas of query-and-clip cards. The outer cards fade and blur, and everything keeps drifting.
- **Constant motion:** nothing on screen is ever completely still.

Our video series uses the same kinds of moves, in our own style: blurred screens behind the main window, a tilted rushing
wall, an empty player and a click, and a camera pull-back over a canvas of searches, episodes or phones.

Note for Vionlabs: this is TwelveLabs positioning against tag-based metadata, which is close to what Vionlabs
sells (M1). Our answer is that Vionlabs metadata comes from what is in the video (mood, emotion, story,
scenes), so the same structure works for us with entertainment-language queries.

## What Vionlabs can take

1. **Hook with the felt problem.** Our Ad 3 video opens on a calm headline. An interruption or
   scrubbing opener would stop the scroll better.
2. **One idea and a few words per video.** Our videos show headline, UI, legend and CTA at once.
3. **Show a click that does something** (add the break, export the clip, add to a list), not only the analysis.
4. **Stock footage avoids the rights question** that the brief raises about real film stills.
5. **Where we go further:** TwelveLabs talks about scene boundaries and "natural pauses". Vionlabs can show
   the story itself (the emotional arc, with breaks in its valleys) and what rides on each break (mood, IAB
   category, brand safety). Our look stays distinct: dark navy and blue against their white and lime.
6. **Formats:** they run 16:9 only. The brief asks for 1:1 and 9:16, which suit LinkedIn mobile feeds better.
