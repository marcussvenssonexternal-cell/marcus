# The catalogue you already own

_Subscription economics · for Tier-1 streamers, Small–mid streamers (niche and regional), Telco / cable operators_

**Streamers fight churn with new content and new bundles. The cheaper lever is the library they already paid for. Every price rise makes viewers ask whether the service was worth it this month, and that question is settled on the home screen.**

## Why now

Disney+ and Hulu raised prices again on 21 October. Antenna finds about 1 in 3 people who cancel a service buy another within 30 days, so the money stays in streaming and just moves. HUB Research says the service a viewer treats as their default is up to 6x harder to cancel. Being the default is won in discovery, not in the content budget.

## Questions the series answers

- How much of a typical catalogue gets watched at all, and what happens to the rest?
- What makes a service someone's default, and can discovery earn it?
- Why do top-10 rows keep sending viewers to what they would have found anyway?
- Cold start: what do you show a viewer who has watched two titles, or a service without Netflix-scale data?
- Which numbers should a CPO track to know discovery is working?
